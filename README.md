# Data Professionals Survey Analysis | Power BI

## Project Overview

This project analyses survey responses from 630 individuals working in or aspiring to enter the data profession.

Using Microsoft Power BI, I developed an interactive dashboard to explore salary differences, job satisfaction, programming language preferences, demographic characteristics and the challenges associated with entering the data industry.

The aim was to transform survey responses into meaningful insights that could help aspiring data professionals understand career trends while providing organisations with information about workforce satisfaction and professional development.

## Project Objectives

- Compare average salaries across data-related professions.
- Examine satisfaction with salary and work-life balance.
- Identify popular programming languages across different job roles.
- Understand respondents' perceptions of entering the data industry.
- Explore demographic and geographical patterns among survey participants.
- Develop an interactive dashboard to communicate the findings clearly.
- Provide practical recommendations for career development and employee retention.

## Dashboard Analysis and Key Findings

### 1. Average Salary by Job Title

**Analysis:** Compared average salaries across different data-related professions.

**Key Finding:** Data Scientists recorded the highest average salary at approximately $103,000, followed by Data Architects and Data Engineers.

**Career Insight:** Specialised technical and analytical positions were associated with higher average earnings among the survey respondents.

This highlights the potential value of developing advanced analytical and technical skills for professionals interested in career progression.

### 2. Happiness with Work Life Balance

**Analysis:** Examined respondents' satisfaction with their work life balance using a scale of 1 to 10.

**Key Finding:** The average work life balance satisfaction score was 5.86/10.

**Business Insight:** Respondents reported moderate satisfaction with their work life balance, indicating opportunities for organisations to improve workload management, flexibility and employee well being.

### 3. Happiness with Salary

**Analysis:** Measured how satisfied participants were with their current earnings.

**Key Finding:** Average salary satisfaction was 4.27/10.

**Business Insight:** Salary satisfaction was relatively low among respondents, suggesting that organisations could benefit from reviewing compensation structures, career progression opportunities and employee expectations.

### 4. Difficulty of Breaking into the Data Industry

**Analysis:** Explored how respondents perceived the difficulty of entering the data profession.

**Key Finding:** 42.7% of participants described entering the industry as neither easy nor difficult, making this the most common response.

**Career Insight:** Respondents expressed varying experiences of entering the industry. Practical training, portfolio development and mentorship may help aspiring professionals navigate the transition into data related roles.

### 5. Favourite Programming Language by Job Title

**Analysis:** Compared programming language preferences across data related professions.

**Key Finding:** Python was the most preferred programming language among the survey respondents and appeared prominently across several job categories.

**Career Insight:** Python is a useful skill for professionals interested in data analytics, automation, machine learning and data science.

### 6. Survey Participants by Country

**Analysis:** Examined the geographical distribution of survey respondents.

**Key Finding:** Major respondent groups came from the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom and India.

**Business Insight:** The dataset captures experiences from respondents in several countries, allowing comparisons across geographical groups.

However, the findings should be interpreted as characteristics of the surveyed participants rather than as representative estimates for the entire global data workforce.

### 7. Average Age of Survey Participants

**Analysis:** Calculated the average age of survey respondents.

**Key Finding:** The average participant age was 29.87 years.

**Career Insight:** The survey includes a substantial presence of people around the early stages of their professional careers, although age alone does not determine career level or experience.

### 8. Survey Participation

**Analysis:** Measured the total number of completed responses included in the dashboard.

**Key Finding:** The dataset contained 630 survey participants.

**Analytical Insight:** This provides a useful sample for exploratory analysis of the participating group. Broader conclusions about the data industry would require consideration of how respondents were recruited and how representative the sample is.

## Interactive Dashboard Features

The dashboard includes five slicers that allow users to explore survey findings across different respondent groups.

| Slicer | Function |
|---|---|
| Month | Explore survey responses by month |
| Level of Education | Compare findings across educational backgrounds |
| Ethnicity | Explore patterns across respondent categories |
| Gender | Compare survey results across gender groups |
| Age Level | Analyse respondents using DAX-created age categories |

These filters allow users to investigate how salaries, satisfaction and preferences differ across selected groups.

## Data Modelling and DAX

The project includes the use of Power BI's data modelling features and DAX-created age categories.

Age groups were developed to allow respondents to be analysed across different age levels instead of relying only on individual ages.

This makes it easier to compare survey patterns across demographic segments and create more accessible visualisations.

The dashboard also demonstrates the use of multiple visuals and interactive filters to summarise survey responses.

## Recommendations

Based on the findings, I developed the following recommendations.

### 1. Encourage Practical Skills Development

Aspiring data professionals should build their skills through practical projects, certifications and portfolio development.

### 2. Strengthen Python Training

Given Python's popularity among the respondents, it is a useful programming language for individuals pursuing analytical and technical careers.

### 3. Review Salary and Compensation Strategies

Organisations should examine salary satisfaction and provide transparent compensation structures and progression opportunities.

### 4. Improve Work Life Balance

Employers should consider flexible working arrangements, realistic workloads and employee well-being initiatives.

### 5. Create Clear Career Development Pathways

Structured training, mentoring and progression opportunities could help employees develop skills for more specialised roles.

### 6. Support Diversity and Inclusion

Demographic analysis can help organisations investigate differences in employee experiences and identify areas where support may be needed.

### 7. Improve Entry Level Career Support

Mentorship, internships and practical training opportunities can help newcomers develop confidence and relevant experience.

### 8. Monitor Workforce Satisfaction

Regular employee surveys can help organisations identify changes in salary satisfaction, work-life balance and professional development needs.

The Data Professionals Survey Dashboard provides insights into salary trends, employee satisfaction, programming language preferences and career experiences among 630 survey respondents.

Data Scientists recorded the highest average salaries, while Python emerged as the most preferred programming language.

At the same time, relatively low salary satisfaction and moderate work life balance scores highlighted areas where organisations could improve employee experience.

The analysis also revealed differences in respondents' perceptions of entering the data industry, demonstrating the importance of practical skill development, career guidance and continuous learning.
